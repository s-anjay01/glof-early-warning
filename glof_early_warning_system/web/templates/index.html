1. Costa (1985) Empirical Peak Discharge:
Q_max = 0.013 × (V_w)^0.598 × (H_d)^0.548
Where V_w is total impounded water volume (m³) and H_d is dam crest height (m).
2. Froehlich (1995) Breach Peak Discharge:
Q_max = 0.607 × (V_w)^0.295 × (H_w)^1.24
H_w is the depth of water above breach invert.
3. Subaerial Impulse Wave (Heller & Hager, 2010):
H_0 = 0.35 × h × (V_ice / h³)^0.32 × sin(α)
Models tsunami displacement wave triggered by hanging ice/rock slope collapse into lake.
4. Mountain Canyon Flood Wave Attenuation & Velocity:
Q(x) = Q_peak / [1 + 0.025 × (x)^0.72] • v = (1/n) × R^(2/3) × S^(1/2)
Accounts for canyon bed friction (Manning n ~ 0.055) and high Himalayan channel gradients (S ~ 0.04).

GLOF Early Warning & Hazard Assessment System is an end-to-end Python & Geospatial platform designed to monitor expanding glacial lakes across the Hindu Kush-Himalayas (Sikkim, Uttarakhand, Nepal, Himachal Pradesh, Bhutan, and Ladakh).

By integrating multi-temporal satellite observations (NDWI / MNDWI), Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) multi-criteria risk scoring, and hydrodynamic dam-break physics, the system provides decision-makers with early warning lead times and quantifiable downstream risk profiles.
